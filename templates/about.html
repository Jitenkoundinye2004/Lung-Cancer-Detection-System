Adenocarcinoma

Type: Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)

Prevalence: Most common type of lung cancer in non-smokers, but also found in smokers.

Origin: Begins in the glandular (mucus-secreting) cells lining the outer parts of the lungs.

Growth Rate: Slower than other lung cancers.

Metastasis Sites: Brain, bones, liver, and adrenal glands.

Symptoms: Persistent cough, shortness of breath, chest pain, fatigue.

Large Cell Carcinoma

Type: Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)

Prevalence: Less common but very aggressive.

Origin: Can occur in any part of the lung and tends to grow and spread quickly.

Subtypes: Sometimes includes large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma.

Symptoms: Rapid weight loss, chest discomfort, hoarseness, coughing up blood.

Squamous Cell Carcinoma

Type: Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)

Prevalence: Strongly associated with smoking.

Origin: Arises in the flat cells lining the airways, usually near the central bronchial tubes.

Growth Rate: Moderate; can form cavities in the lung.

Symptoms: Cough with phlegm, wheezing, frequent respiratory infections, chest pain.

Normal

Meaning: Images in this class represent healthy lung tissue.

Use Case: Used as control data for model training and comparison.

Appearance: Clear alveolar structures, no signs of tumor mass or cellular anomalies.
